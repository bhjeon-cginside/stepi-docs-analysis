2. National, Economic and Technology Security Strategies of the United States

Foreign investment regulation laws, such as FIRRMA, are not for the unconditional ban of foreign investment and should be understood as countermeasures for the exponential increase of Chinese investment in the U.S. from a national security perspective (Kim, 2020).

![](<[STI Policy Review-1]STI Policy Review - Dec 2022 Vol.1_images/imageFile2.png>)

However, due to the accelerating global economic crisis and the demand from U.S. industries to deregulate, it is necessary to take time and evaluate whether such foreign investment regulations will function properly.

![](<[STI Policy Review-1]STI Policy Review - Dec 2022 Vol.1_images/imageFile3.png>)

- Some industries, such as bio companies, are pointing out that the range and the criteria of TID is too broad, asking for an adjustment (Kim, 2020).

The United States, with the Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917, began using a trade sanction strategy in its competition with other states and is now focusing on restraining China with Entity List and the Export Control Reform Act.

![](<[STI Policy Review-1]STI Policy Review - Dec 2022 Vol.1_images/imageFile4.png>)

![](<[STI Policy Review-1]STI Policy Review - Dec 2022 Vol.1_images/imageFile5.png>)

Trading with the Enemy Act of 1917 (TWEA): TWEA grants the authority to the president of the U.S. to supervise and restrict trade between the U.S. and the enemy states and is the basis of the trade restrictions on other countries.

![](<[STI Policy Review-1]STI Policy Review - Dec 2022 Vol.1_images/imageFile6.png>)

- TWEA is a law that grants the authority to the U.S. president to supervise and restrict all trades between the U.S. and the enemy states in case of a war.
- Emergency Banking Act of 1933 expanded the authority from wartime to times of peace.


Entity List: Entity List, which did not receive much attention in its early stage, has become a key strategy to restrain Chinese enterprises as the US-China strategic competition accelerates.

![](<[STI Policy Review-1]STI Policy Review - Dec 2022 Vol.1_images/imageFile7.png>)

- The Entity List, a list of enterprises subject to export controls maintained and managed by the Bureau of Industry and Security in the Department of Commerce, received little attention in the beginning as the policymakers tended to focus on sanctions from the Department of the Treasury and State.
